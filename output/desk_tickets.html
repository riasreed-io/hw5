Reflection

Homework 5, Problem 10. My read on the agent team after the 2026-10-14 run, using the ticket tabs and the cash tab on this page as the evidence.

1 · How I would evaluate the agents on each ticket

They would have to be accurate, efficient, and do it by only calling the right agents. It has to be super super accurate on the financials and quick to answer. It also has to address the specifics of the ticket — all aspects of it. That is how I would evaluate it, and here is what each of those looks like against the three tickets I actually ran.

Accurate

Every number the agents reported came back matching the database. On ticket 101 Inventory reported size S at 0 on hand against the 1 the customer wanted, and that is exactly what inventory says. On 103 it reported 8 of 20 in size M — 12 short — and that is right too. Nobody invented a size, a vendor, or a date. The one date that got quoted, 2026-09-05 for the reprint, came from check_vendor_can_ship adding Bulldog Print Co's 5-day lead to the shop's own date, and Customer Service was careful to call it an estimate rather than a promise. That is the standard: if I cannot find the number in the database, the agent should not have said it.

Super accurate on the financials

This is the one I care most about, and it is the one the run did best. Look at the cash tab: 3,400.00 → 2,560.00 → 160.00, and the ending balance matches cash_accounts exactly. The 840.00 Accounting asked for on ticket 101 is exactly what invoice 501 says is owed, and the 2,400.00 on ticket 102 is exactly the lease. Neither was rounded, estimated, or bundled. Both payments are sitting in payments stamped approved by Rias Reed, not by an agent. If the cash tab did not reconcile to the penny I would call the whole run a failure even with three green Resolved chips on the board, because a shop that cannot trust its own balance cannot use any of the rest of it.

Only calling the right agents

Ticket 102 is the clean example of this working: one delegation, Boss to Accounting, nothing else, and Facilities, Inventory and Customer Service all stayed asleep. Tickets 101 and 103 pulled in three specialists each, which I think is fair given both of them were blocked on two different things at once — stock and money. The measure I would use is whether every agent that got woken up actually contributed something to the answer. On 103, Customer Service was called and then could not write anything, because no price had been agreed. That is a wasted wake-up, and it is the kind of thing I would tighten.

Quick

Ticket 102 resolved in one pass and 20,550 tokens. Ticket 103 took 69,725 tokens — more than three times as much — and still did not produce a price. Ticket 101 needed three passes and 159,525 tokens before it closed. So "quick" is not the same for all three, and the spread is the interesting part: the ticket with one specialist was the fastest and the cleanest, and the ticket with the most back-and-forth cost the most and delivered the least.

Addressing all aspects of the ticket

This is where I would mark them down. Ticket 103 asked two things — can we do 20 in M, and can we do a bulk discount — and the run answered the stock half (12 short) but closed without a price, because the Yale AI Club never named one and Accounting would not invent one. Ticket 101 ended with a draft to Tauhid that was written but not sent, so the customer still has not actually heard anything. Both are defensible, and both are also unfinished from the customer's point of view. A ticket that resolves with the work recorded is better than one that resolves with a guess, but it is not the same as done.

2 · How the actual run compared to the plan I wrote

101 — Bulldog tee

I got it mostly right, but the order of it was different, so it did call on Customer Service after all — that was wrong with my prediction. I said Accounting first because the invoice has to be paid; the Boss went to Inventory first on all three passes, to establish there was no stock at all before touching the money.

102 — Rent due

The tools I predicted did actually get used, but I was wrong about Facilities being called on, which was overlooked by me initially. I named request_payment and list_money_due and those are exactly what ran — but they ran inside Accounting, and Facilities never woke up at all on its own ticket.

103 — Bulk hoodie discount

I got the right agents and inventory tools, but I did miss the order and Customer Service being called altogether, as well as the list_payment_requests tool, so that was also miscalculated on my end for the prediction. The Boss led with Accounting and quote_discount rather than Inventory, and list_payment_requests never ran because no payment ever came up on this ticket.

The pattern underneath all three

I predicted who owns the subject. The Boss routed by what blocks the ticket. On 101 the blocker was stock before money, so Inventory led. On 102 the blocker was cash, so Accounting led and Facilities was redundant. On 103 the blocker was a price nobody had named, so Accounting led. It did surprise me how well it actually ran on its own — it is quite proficient. I would change the prompts to be more systematic about how it is going to decide on calling agents, so the structure is quite clear and there isn't some guessing, and especially no mistakes: read the ticket, name the blocker, call the agent who owns that. I think the token usage is okay for a first few runs and it will get more efficient over time — though the honest version of that is that the agents have no memory between runs, so the efficiency has to come from me rewriting the prompts, not from them learning.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

I think it would have been simpler with some tasks and a full range of tools. Some of these tickets aren't all that complex, so it seems like they could be handled by one agent.

Ticket 102 is the proof. The entire run was Boss → Accounting → list_money_due → request_payment → resolve_ticket. That is one agent's worth of work with four tools, wrapped in a five-agent team. The delegation added a whole extra model conversation — the Boss writing a question, Accounting reading it, Accounting writing a report, the Boss reading it — to a ticket whose answer was "the rent is 2,400.00, it is due in two days, we have 2,560.00, raise the request."

The waste shows up in the trail in two concrete ways. First, list_open_tickets gets called over and over by different agents in the same run, because each specialist re-reads the board instead of being handed what the Boss already read. Second, the depth limit fired several times per ticket — agents trying to chain further than two hops and being refused. One agent holding all ten tools would not re-read anything and could not delegate in a circle.

Where I would keep the team is ticket 101 and 103, where two genuinely different kinds of expertise collide: a stock question and a money question that block each other. Even then, the honest split is probably three agents — a Boss, an operations agent covering stock and vendors, and a money agent — rather than five. Facilities never woke up once across the whole run, which is a strong hint that it did not need to exist as its own agent for this shop.

4 · Three new problems this team could solve with the tools I built

A multi-item inventory inquiry across several orders

Somebody asks whether we can cover four different items at once — say the tee in S, the hoodie in L, the cap, and the mug. The tools already handle this: check_stock works per sku and size and returns the shortfall, and check_vendor_can_ship tells you who restocks each one and whether they are blocked. It is ticket 101's work repeated four times. The one thing that makes it interesting is that CC-MUG-CREST is at 0 and routes to a different vendor (Elm City Gifts, 3-day lead) than the apparel, so Inventory would have to come back with two different restock dates in one answer.

A bulk quote where the customer actually names a price

This is ticket 103 with the missing piece supplied. The run stalled because the Yale AI Club never said what they wanted to pay, and Accounting would not invent a number. If a customer writes "we want 20 hoodies at 40 each," quote_discount answers it immediately: against a 22.00 cost and 58.00 list, 40.00 is a 31% discount off list leaving 18.00 margin a unit and 360.00 on the order, comfortably above the cost floor. Accounting could approve or counter it on the spot, and Customer Service could draft the reply, without a single new tool.

A size change before the order is placed

A customer wants a certain size, then changes their mind and wants a different size before they place the order. Tauhid on ticket 101 is one message away from exactly this: he wants S, which is at 0, and the draft already offers him M, L or XL, which are at 5, 3 and 2. If he replies "make it M," the team needs check_stock to confirm the M, update_inventory to take one off the shelf with a reason on the record, save_draft_message to confirm back to him, and resolve_ticket to close it. All four already exist, and no money moves, so no approval is needed.

5 · Three problems this team could not solve, and what it would take

A refund

They could not handle a refund. Everything in this shop is built to move cash one direction: approve_and_pay subtracts from checking, and the assignment even says cash only goes out because revenue was never modelled. There is no tool that puts money back, and no record of the customer ever having paid in the first place — payments only holds what the shop paid out, both rows of it (840.00 and 2,400.00) going to a vendor and a landlord. To do it I would need a refund_payment tool that writes a credit and increases cash_accounts.balance, a customer-orders table to refund against, and the same human approval gate pointed the other way — a refund is money moving, so no agent should be able to release one either. I would also want Inventory in the loop through check_inventory so we can offer an alternative to the order the customer does not want any more, rather than just handing the money back.

A wrong order that gets returned

I don't think the tools can handle a wrong order that they return as well. It needs everything the refund needs, plus the physical side. Right now update_inventory can put a unit back on the shelf, but nothing records that a return is in transit, nothing issues a shipping label, and nothing tracks whether the item actually arrived before the money goes back. I would need tools to handle shipping, returns, refunding and apologising — a start_return that opens an RMA, a receive_return that confirms the item is physically back and only then restocks it, a shipping tool for the label and the cost, and the refund tool above. The team would also need a Returns or Logistics agent, because that work is not Inventory's counting job and not Accounting's ledger job — it sits between them, and on this run neither one would have picked it up.

Somebody offering to buy the shop

I don't think they could handle something where someone wanted to purchase or acquire the company. Nothing in this database can answer whether an offer is good. There is one cash account at 160.00, one lease, three vendors and four SKUs — no revenue, no profit and loss, no history, no idea what the brand or the Chapel Street location is worth. Accounting's entire financial picture is "what do we owe and can we cover it," which is the wrong question entirely. I would need tools that assess the company's performance against the offer: revenue and margin history over time, a valuation tool, the full obligation picture including the remaining lease term, and something to model the offer against all of it. And I would need a new agent for it — a Finance or Strategy agent — because this is a decision about the shop's future rather than about a ticket on the board, and the Boss as written is built to clear tickets, not to sell the business.
